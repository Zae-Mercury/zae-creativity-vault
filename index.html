2024 · Biomedicine

CARRAVER

MAN 4 Jakarta

CARAVERR: Combination of Papaya Peel Extract (Carica papaya L.) and Belimbing Wuluh Extract (Averrhoa bilimbi L.) as Antimicrobial in the Form of Therapeutic Lozenges. CARAVERR investigates whether combining papaya peel and sour star fruit extracts can create an effective antimicrobial therapeutic lozenge for upper respiratory tract infections. Using ethanol maceration, phytochemical screening confirmed the presence of alkaloids, flavonoids, tannins, phenols, and saponins, all linked to antibacterial activity. Antimicrobial testing with the disc diffusion method against Staphylococcus aureus showed measurable inhibition, with Formulation 2 performing best. Physical and organoleptic evaluations demonstrated good hardness, dissolving time, and high panelist acceptance. Overall, the study shows that fruit‑waste–based extracts can be formulated into safe, functional lozenges with promising antibacterial potential.

2023 · Material Engineering

SUGAPI

MAN 4 Jakarta

SUGAPI: Sustainable Carbon Fiber from Sugarcane Bagasse—A Renewable Alternative to Petroleum-Based Precursors. The conventional production of carbon fiber, reliant on petroleum-based precursors such as polyacrylonitrile (PAN) and pitch, poses significant challenges, including high energy consumption, exorbitant manufacturing costs, and limited sustainability due to finite fossil fuel resources. This research addresses these issues by exploring an innovative approach to carbon fiber production utilizing sugar cane bagasse, an underutilized agricultural waste product. The primary objective of this study is to investigate the feasibility of transforming sugar cane bagasse into carbon fiber, evaluating its environmental and economic viability. By harnessing bagasse's renewable nature, carbon fiber production can significantly reduce its carbon footprint and energy requirements. Furthermore, the research aims to analyze the potential cost-effectiveness of this approach compared to traditional methods, taking advantage of the lower cost of the feedstock and the potentially less energy-intensive conversion processes.
